Argos

Algorithmic trading systems built on an anti-overfitting validation protocol.

Overview

Most profitable-looking backtests are statistical mirages, and the people running them rarely know it. Argos, named after Argos Panoptes, the hundred-eyed watchman of Greek myth, is a personal research project built around one idea: a validation protocol designed to kill those mirages. Nothing slips past it, and only what survives the full protocol ever reaches execution with money, which for now is simulated.

The study covers seven years of market data from 2019 to 2026, more than 180 assets across US and European equities plus crypto, and tens of millions of price bars. Developed in collaboration with Claude as a research, engineering and documentation copilot; direction, judgement and decisions by the author.

The validation protocol

Every candidate strategy goes through the same funnel, defined before looking at any results.

Pre-registered hypothesis

Economic mechanism, falsifiable predictions and rejection criteria written down before the first number.

Cross-universe validation

Designed on asset group A, required to work on an untouched twin group B, then on a third universe downloaded on purpose.

One-look temporal judge

The most recent data segment is consulted exactly once, at the very end. Looking at it twice invalidates it.

Monte Carlo

Thousands of resamples to separate skill from luck, including a custom variant for compounding strategies.

Survivorship audit

Re-tested after adding companies that actually went bankrupt to the universe, 2023 fallen banks included.

Forward test

Nothing counts as validated until it runs for months in real time, automated on a broker's simulated account.

Results: two survivors out of nine families

System 1, US equity swing

Buys pullbacks within uptrends over a multi-day horizon, with a market-regime filter. Backtest 2021 to 2026 with friction included: CAGR around 21%, profit factor 1.25, maximum drawdown around -34%. Passed three universes and the temporal judge; Monte Carlo probability of loss around 1%. Now in automated forward test.

System 2, crypto trend

Buys strength breakouts around the clock, with a regime switch that turns the bot off during crypto winters. Backtest: CAGR around 60%, profit factor 1.85, maximum drawdown around -29%. The twin universe performed better than the design one; Monte Carlo probability of loss around 0%. Now in automated forward test.

Historical simulation figures with pessimistic costs. Not real returns, and not a promise of future returns. Drawdown is part of the price.

Hypotheses buried with data

The invisible work that gives the rest its value. Intraday trading, three systems, died mathematically: a study on 69,000 observations showed the maximum signal available within the day is smaller than the cost of trading. Short selling, nine variants across three generations, was negative across the board; in the universe studied weakness bounces, so selling dips equals buying squeezes. Momentum rotation on dividend stocks lost to its own passive equal-weight basket. And a system with spectacular design-group metrics, +9.9% per trade, turned out to be a statistical artifact that collapsed in both control universes, which is exactly what the protocol exists for.

Along the way seven methodology bugs were hunted down: results selected alphabetically instead of by date, standard Monte Carlo applied to compounded series producing impossible -195% drawdowns, inflated CAGR denominators caught through external review with another AI model, survivorship bias quantified at -6.5% instead of assumed, and paper leverage no real account would allow under the Reg-T legal limit, with its hidden margin cost estimated.

Infrastructure

Python backtesting framework

Custom built: pessimistic friction, look-ahead prevention, portfolio simulation, metrics including profit factor, CAGR, drawdown and deflated Sharpe.

Monte Carlo suite

Bootstrap, permutation, drop and stress tests with charts, plus the custom compounding variant.

Autonomous execution bots

Run against a broker REST API on a simulated account: scheduled orders, broker-persisted stops, state reconciliation, unattended scheduling, portable across Windows and macOS.

Documentation

More than twelve technical reports in PDF and markdown with a dated decision log. Every change to every system has a date, a reason and a result.

Lessons learned

Friction and cost matter more than signal, so measure them first. If you do not pre-register the hypothesis, the backtest will tell you what you want to hear. Validation on virgin data is not optional: it is the test. Losses arrive in clusters because regimes cluster, and statistical medians underestimate the real pain. Burying seven ideas with data is worth more than falling in love with one without it.

Personal research project for educational purposes. Nothing here constitutes financial advice. Method and parameters are not public; full detail available on request.

Social Media Audit Bot

Automated social media account analyser and report generator.

Overview

This automated agent reviews social media accounts, systematically analysing titles, portraits, content quality and engagement metrics such as comments and likes.

By comparing the target account with similar successful content in the same niche, the bot generates actionable, data-driven reports designed to help creators grow their views and followers. It is actively sold on Fiverr.

The Morning Club

Financial news aggregation platform with AI sentiment analysis.

Overview

The Morning Club streamlined financial news consumption. Using AI models, it aggregated, summarised and analysed the sentiment of daily business news, so a reader stayed informed without spending hours on articles.

It focused on key tech companies such as NVIDIA and AMD, providing daily metric reports and actionable insight. It also ran an educational community forum where users discussed trends and shared knowledge.

The Morning Club has been discontinued. A huge thank-you to the more than 3,000 people who pre-registered and tested the product. Your sign-ups, feedback and enthusiasm shaped every part of it.

Astralis

Multi-model AI assistant with voice synthesis and automation.

Overview

Astralis was a conversational assistant built to automate daily tasks. Integrating multiple AI models through OpenRouter and n8n, it handled complex queries, managed calendars and email, and scraped web data.

Its standout feature was voice synthesis powered by ElevenLabs, plus a call capability that let it phone real numbers and speak, to make reservations or ask for details.

Discontinued: better options now exist on the market. It stays in the portfolio as a demonstration of complex API integration and automation.
